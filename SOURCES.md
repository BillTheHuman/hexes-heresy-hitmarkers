## United States statutes and regulation

- [17 U.S.C. § 102](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/102) — identifies
  copyrightable subject matter. Section 102(b) excludes ideas, procedures,
  processes, systems, methods of operation, concepts, principles, and
  discoveries. That exclusion does not categorically remove protection from
  expressive code, audiovisual material, or an original selection or
  arrangement.
- [17 U.S.C. § 106](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/106) — identifies
  the copyright owner's exclusive rights, including reproduction, adaptation,
  and distribution.
- [17 U.S.C. § 107](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/107) — requires a
  case-specific four-factor fair-use analysis. Research, scholarship,
  criticism, and comment are examples rather than categorical safe harbors.
- [17 U.S.C. § 117](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/117) — contains
  narrow alternative provisions for an owner of a copy of a computer program:
  section 117(a)(1) concerns an essential-step copy or adaptation used in no
  other manner, while section 117(a)(2) concerns an archival-only copy. Section
  117(b) separately restricts transfer. Section 117 is not a general reverse-
  engineering or research safe harbor.
- [17 U.S.C. § 201](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/201) — addresses
  initial copyright ownership, including work-made-for-hire and other statutory
  ownership rules.
- [17 U.S.C. § 202](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/202) — distinguishes
  ownership of a cartridge or other material object from ownership of copyright
  in the work embodied in it.
- [17 U.S.C. § 204](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/204) — generally
  requires a signed writing for a transfer of copyright ownership other than by
  operation of law. It does not imply that every permission or nonexclusive
  license transfers ownership.
- [17 U.S.C. § 1201](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/1201) — separately
  regulates circumvention and trafficking. Subsection (f)(1) applies only to a
  person who lawfully obtained the right to use a copy, and only to
  circumvention undertaken solely to identify and analyze previously not readily
  available elements necessary for interoperability of an independently created
  program. Subsections (f)(2) and (f)(3) separately constrain circumvention means
  and disclosure by necessity, interoperability-only purpose, noninfringement,
  and other applicable law. This report does not conclude that static
  recompilation satisfies those conditions.
- [37 C.F.R. § 201.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-201/section-201.40)
  — contains temporary classes exempting specified qualifying acts from the
  section 1201(a)(1)(A) circumvention prohibition. Paragraph (b)(19)(i) applies
  to complete games lawfully acquired when the copyright owner or its
  authorized representative has ceased providing access to an external server
  necessary for authentication; paragraph (b)(19)(i)(A) permits copying and
  modification solely to restore personal, local gameplay. Paragraph
  (b)(19)(ii) applies only to qualifying preservation by an eligible library,
  archives, or museum of a lawfully acquired complete offline game no longer
  reasonably available commercially. It is not a general private-owner game-
  preservation exemption, does not authorize trafficking, and does not decide
  copyright, contract, or other liability. No coverage is asserted here. The
  current eCFR text was checked on September 27, 2026.
- [2024 final rule, 89 Fed. Reg. 85437 — official PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-10-28/pdf/2024-24563.pdf)
  — publishes the current triennial section 1201 exemption rule, effective
  October 28, 2024.
- [18 U.S.C. § 1839](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/1839) — defines a
  federal trade secret and “improper means.” Reverse engineering, independent
  derivation, and other lawful acquisition are excluded from “improper means,”
  but that exclusion does not establish lawful access, erase a secrecy or use
  duty, preempt state remedies, or resolve contract questions.
- [15 U.S.C. § 1125](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/1125) — addresses
  false association, likelihood of confusion, and dilution. The treatment of
  product names, character names, and disclaimers is jurisdiction- and fact-
  specific.

## Selected judicial authorities and official summaries

The Copyright Office links below are official case summaries, not judicial
opinions. They are research aids and do not replace review of the full opinions,
controlling jurisdiction, and subsequent treatment.

- *Sega Enterprises Ltd. v. Accolade, Inc.*, 977 F.2d 1510 (9th Cir. 1992) —
  [Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/segaenters-accolade-9thcir1992.pdf).
  The court found intermediate copying fair on the particular record where
  disassembly was needed to reach unprotected interface information and the
  resulting games were independently written. The opinion also treated section
  117 as a narrow provision that did not itself authorize the disassembly at
  issue.
- *Sony Computer Entertainment, Inc. v. Connectix Corp.*, 203 F.3d 596 (9th
  Cir. 2000) —
  [Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf).
  Intermediate copying used to create a new, noninfringing compatibility
  product was fair on those facts.
- *Atari Games Corp. v. Nintendo of America Inc.*, 975 F.2d 832 (Fed. Cir.
  1992) —
  [Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/atari-nintendo-fedcir1992.pdf).
  The court treated examination of object code in rightful possession as
  permissible intermediate reverse engineering, but held that copying an
  improperly obtained source-code copy did not qualify as fair use and that
  similarities beyond compatibility necessities supported likely infringement.
- *Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc.*, 593 U.S. 1 (2021) —
  [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/18-956_new_3fb4.pdf).
  The Court assumed copyrightability and held limited copying of API declaring
  code fair on the specific record; it did not announce a general rule for
  software or whole-program translation.
- *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340
  (1991) —
  [official U.S. Reports opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-499/pdf/USREPORTS-499-340.pdf).
  Facts are not copyrightable, although an original selection or arrangement
  may be.
- *MDY Industries, LLC v. Blizzard Entertainment, Inc.*, 629 F.3d 928 (9th Cir.
  2010), amended February 17, 2011 —
  [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/02/17/09-15932.pdf).
  In the Ninth Circuit, an infringement or fair-use analysis does not by itself
  resolve section 1201 access-control liability. The court did not decide the
  defendant's section 1201(f) theory on the merits because it had been waived.
- *Jack Daniel's Properties, Inc. v. VIP Products LLC*, 599 U.S. 140 (2023) —
  [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/22-148_3e04.pdf).
  When an accused designation is used as a source identifier, ordinary
  likelihood-of-confusion analysis applies; expressive-use doctrines and
  dilution exclusions are not blanket immunity.

*Sega* and *Connectix* concern intermediate copying used to develop independent
compatible products. They do not establish that distributing mechanically
translated whole-program material is fair use.

## Official explanatory and AI-authorship material

- [U.S. Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/) —
  explains the four-factor framework and provides a selective case index.
- [U.S. Copyright Office Section 1201](https://www.copyright.gov/1201/) —
  collects official information on the rulemaking and current exemptions.
- [Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)
  — states the Copyright Office's current view that AI assistance does not bar
  protection for identifiable human-authored contributions, while prompting
  alone ordinarily does not provide sufficient human control. Human selection,
  arrangement, and modification are evaluated case by case.
- [Copyright Registration Guidance: Works Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf)
  — tells registration applicants to disclose more-than-de-minimis AI-generated
  material and limit a registration claim to human-authored contributions. It
  is registration guidance, not a general publication-disclosure statute.
- *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, 130 F.4th 1039 (D.C. Cir. 2025) —
  [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2025/03/23-5233.pdf).
  A machine identified as sole author cannot be an author under the Copyright
  Act. The decision did not resolve copyrightability of a particular mixed
  human-and-AI work. The Supreme Court
  [denied certiorari on March 2, 2026](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-449.html);
  the denial expresses no view on the merits.
